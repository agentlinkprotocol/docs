---
title: "Why the Atlas cannot be captured"
description: "Four pillars that reinforce each other. None is sufficient alone; all are verifiable by anyone."
---

Bitcoin holds because attacking costs more, continuously, than it yields, and because the network's history is public
and unforgeable. The Atlas has neither token nor mining, but takes both ideas with four mechanisms.

## Pillar 1: positions are not chosen

A node's position is the hash of its key, a one-way function. Nobody can place themselves where they want in the data
space, and others recompute the position rather than trusting the claim. A small proof of work (20 bits, a few seconds)
only prevents creating identities on the fly to flood connections. **It is not the security mechanism.**

## Pillar 2: identity is earned with time

A node's weight (routing priority, guardian order, publication quota) is its **service seniority**: the number of days,
within the last 365, during which it answered at least 90 % of its challenges. Challenges are drawn by common randomness
(drand rounds): for each node and round, three controllers are designated by hash, and anyone can recompute who had the
right to challenge whom. Seniority is non-transferable and cannot be accelerated. An attacker who wants 10 000 influential
nodes must run 10 000 real servers, with real storage, for months, answering honestly. Security costs time and disks,
not CPU.

## Pillar 3: every answer is a signed receipt

Each answer to `store`, `search`, `card`, `challenge`, `sync`, `inbox`, `vault` or `ledger` carries a receipt signed by
the guardian: request hash, timestamp, outcome, hash of the answer. Whoever receives a bad answer holds **proof**, not an
opinion. Reputation and quotas are computed from evidence anyone can verify. Cost: a 64-byte signature per answer.

## Pillar 4: the ledger never forgets

Public facts of the network (operator certificates, revocations, challenge failures observed by controllers, map
version changes) are appended to a Merkle ledger inspired by Certificate Transparency. Every node holds its own copy,
roots are exchanged every 30 seconds, and a root witnessed by many senior nodes cannot be rewritten. Anyone can audit
an operator's history.

## What this gives

A dishonest node can at worst hide a piece of data (there are 20 copies) or lie once (the receipt proves it, the ledger
keeps it, its reputation drops). It cannot forge a card, choose its position, or rewrite the past.

## Also

- **Guardians recompute vectors** from trait text; nobody can inject a misleading vector to land in a zone.
- **Diversity**: at most 2 guardians per key per operator and per /16 (IPv4) or /48 (IPv6). No actor holds all copies.
- **Local, weighted reputation**: opinions from others are weighted by the trust already placed in them (EigenTrust style).
  A hundred fresh Sybil nodes rating each other stay at zero.
- **Query privacy (v1, minimal)**: 2 decoy zones are queried alongside the 4 real ones. v2: secret-shared queries.
